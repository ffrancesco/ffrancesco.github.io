Learning Objectives

  • Use the Reynolds transport theorem to convert system laws to control-volume form
  • Apply integral mass conservation
  • Apply the steady linear-momentum equation
  • Construct a complete fluid free-body diagram
  • Relate the engineering energy equation to Bernoulli
Read the outcomes briefly. Emphasize that students will use the concepts in calculations, not only recognize definitions.

Why a Control Volume?

System

Fixed identity of matter.

Control volume

Selected region with mass, momentum, and energy crossing its boundary.

Use a pipe bend or nozzle to show why following the same particles is inconvenient. The control volume stays attached to the engineering device.

General Balance Pattern

$$\text{Accumulation} + \text{Net outward transport} = \text{Applied source}$$
  • Mass: source is zero
  • Linear momentum: source is resultant external force
  • Energy: sources include heat and work
Repeat this common pattern throughout the lecture. It reduces the appearance that continuity, momentum, and energy are unrelated formulas.

Reynolds Transport Theorem

Connect a law written for a system to a finite control volume.

Reynolds Transport Theorem

For extensive property \(B\), with \(b=B/m\):

$$\frac{dB_{sys}}{dt}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{CV}\rho b\,dV+\int_{CS}\rho b(\mathbf{V}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,dA$$
  • First term: storage inside the control volume
  • Second term: net outward transport across the control surface
Define the outward normal. A negative dot product represents inflow. For this course, focus on fixed non-deforming control volumes.

Mass Conservation

Set the transported extensive property equal to mass.

Integral Continuity Equation

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{CV}\rho\,dV+\int_{CS}\rho\mathbf{V}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dA=0$$

For steady one-dimensional ports:

$$\sum_{in}\dot m=\sum_{out}\dot m$$

For constant density, \(\dot m=\rho Q=\rho AV\).

Connect this general result to A1V1=A2V2 from Lecture 2. Clarify that incompressible does not automatically mean steady.

Continuity Check

At a steady junction, one inlet supplies two outlets:

$$\rho_1A_1V_1=\rho_2A_2V_2+\rho_3A_3V_3$$

For one incompressible fluid, density cancels.

Give students a short numerical flow-rate calculation. Require a clearly drawn control volume and arrows.

Linear Momentum

Use Newton's second law to calculate fluid-induced forces.

Integral Momentum Equation

$$\sum\mathbf{F}_{ext}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{CV}\rho\mathbf{V}\,dV+\int_{CS}\rho\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{V}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,dA$$

For steady one-dimensional ports:

$$\sum\mathbf{F}_{ext}=\sum_{out}\dot m\mathbf{V}-\sum_{in}\dot m\mathbf{V}$$
Momentum is a vector. Write separate x and y balances. Clearly distinguish the force on the fluid from the equal and opposite force exerted by the fluid on the pipe or support.

Force Inventory

  • Pressure forces at ports
  • Weight of fluid in the control volume
  • Wall or support reaction
  • Other applied forces
Do not omit atmospheric pressure inconsistently. Either work with absolute pressure everywhere or use gauge pressure so exposed atmospheric contributions cancel.

Worked Example: 90° Pipe Bend

  1. Draw the control volume and coordinate axes
  2. Apply continuity to find port velocities
  3. Draw the free-body diagram of the fluid
  4. Include pressure, weight, and wall reaction
  5. Apply x- and y-momentum balances
  6. Reverse the sign to obtain force of fluid on the bend
Complete a numerical solution live. Ask students to predict the force direction first. This is the highest-priority structural application in Lecture 3.

Energy and Bernoulli

Position Bernoulli as a restricted mechanical-energy relation.

Engineering Energy Equation

$$\frac{p_1}{\rho g}+\alpha_1\frac{V_1^2}{2g}+z_1+h_p-h_t-h_L=\frac{p_2}{\rho g}+\alpha_2\frac{V_2^2}{2g}+z_2$$
  • \(h_p\): pump head added
  • \(h_t\): turbine head removed
  • \(h_L\): irreversible head loss
  • \(\alpha\): kinetic-energy correction factor
Compare term by term with Bernoulli. Explain that the equation is normally applied between control-volume ports using average velocities.

Lecture 3A Takeaways

  • The Reynolds transport theorem supplies one common control-volume structure
  • Continuity conserves mass
  • Momentum analysis converts velocity change into resultant external force
  • A correct fluid free-body diagram is essential
  • The engineering energy equation extends Bernoulli to real devices
