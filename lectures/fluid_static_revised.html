Lecture 1B · Fluid Statics and Structural Loading

Francesco Ferri

AAU BUILD
Second part of Lecture 1. Target time: 70 minutes including a worked gate problem.

Learning Objectives

  • Derive the pressure distribution in a static fluid
  • Use gauge and absolute pressure consistently
  • Solve a multi-fluid manometer problem
  • Calculate hydrostatic resultant force and center of pressure
  • Interpret hydrostatic pressure as a structural distributed load
Read the outcomes briefly. Emphasize that students will use the concepts in calculations, not only recognize definitions.

Pressure at a Point

A static fluid has no shear stress.

A shrinking fluid wedge gives:

$$p_x=p_y=p_z$$

Pressure is isotropic at a point in a static fluid.

Use this as a concise Pascal-wedge argument. Do not spend time on all intermediate algebra unless students ask. Isotropic means independent of direction at a point.

Static Pressure Field

Move from pressure at a point to pressure variation in space.

Differential Fluid Element

Net pressure force per unit volume:

$$-\nabla p$$

Body force per unit volume:

$$\rho\mathbf{g}$$
Point out the minus sign: pressure force acts from high toward low pressure. This corrects the sign inconsistency in the original deck.

Hydrostatic Equilibrium

Newton's second law for a fluid at rest:

$$-\nabla p+\rho\mathbf{g}=0$$

With \(z\) positive upward and \(\mathbf{g}=-g\mathbf{k}\):

$$\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}=0,\qquad\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}=0,\qquad\frac{dp}{dz}=-\rho g$$
Relate this directly to structural equilibrium: sum of forces equals zero. Pressure is constant horizontally in a connected fluid at rest when gravity is the only body force.

Constant-Density Fluid

Integrating between two elevations:

$$p_2-p_1=\rho g(z_1-z_2)$$
$$p=p_0+\rho gh$$

Assumptions: static fluid, constant density, uniform gravity.

Define h as positive depth below the reference free surface. Ask whether pressure changes with tank shape. It does not, if depth and fluid are the same.

Pressure Measurement

Apply the hydrostatic relation along connected fluid columns.

Manometer Procedure

  1. Start from a known pressure
  2. Add \(\rho g\Delta h\) when moving downward
  3. Subtract \(\rho g\Delta h\) when moving upward
  4. Pressure is continuous across an interface
  5. Equal elevations in the same connected static fluid have equal pressure
Work one two-fluid U-tube example on the board. Require units at every stage. Avoid memorized sign conventions disconnected from the physical path.

Quick Manometer Check

If the right fluid column rises, which side initially had the larger pressure?

Answer: the pressure was larger on the side that depressed its adjacent column.

Use this conceptual question before calculation. Students should infer direction before substituting numbers.

Hydrostatic Loads on Structures

Connect the pressure field to distributed loading and statically equivalent resultants.

Resultant Force on a Plane Surface

For a plane area in a constant-density static liquid:

$$F_R=\int_A p\,dA=\rho g h_c A$$

\(h_c\) is the vertical depth of the area centroid.

Assumptions: gauge pressure at a free surface open to atmosphere, constant density, plane area.

Show that the magnitude equals pressure at the centroid times area. Distinguish the area centroid from the center of pressure.

Center of Pressure

For a vertical plane surface:

$$h_p=h_c+\frac{I_G}{A h_c}$$
  • \(I_G\): second moment of area about the horizontal centroidal axis
  • For a vertical rectangle: \(I_G=bH^3/12\)
  • Usually \(h_p>h_c\)
Explain physically why the resultant acts below the centroid: pressure is larger at greater depth. Mention that inclined surfaces require the general inclination form.

Worked Structural Example: Vertical Gate

  1. Draw the pressure diagram
  2. Locate the area centroid and determine \(h_c\)
  3. Calculate \(F_R=\rho g h_c A\)
  4. Calculate \(h_p\)
  5. Replace the distributed load by \(F_R\) at \(h_p\)
  6. Use structural equilibrium to find support reactions
Complete a numerical example live. Suggested geometry: a 2 m wide by 3 m high vertical gate with its top 1 m below the free surface. Ask students to predict whether the force location is above or below the centroid before calculating.

Dimensional Homogeneity Check

Check the dimensions of the hydrostatic force relation:

$$F_R=\rho g h_c A$$

$$[F_R]=(ML^{-3})(LT^{-2})(L)(L^2)=MLT^{-2}$$

The relation is dimensionally homogeneous.

Use this as the dimensional-analysis component of Lecture 1. Emphasize that dimensional consistency is necessary but does not prove physical correctness.

Lecture 1 Takeaways

  • Static pressure is isotropic at a point
  • Pressure increases linearly with depth for constant density
  • Manometers apply the same hydrostatic relation through connected columns
  • Hydrostatic pressure is a distributed structural load
  • The center of pressure is generally below the area centroid
Exit question: A vertical gate is fully submerged. Is the resultant located at its centroid? Require a physical explanation, not only a formula.

Buoyancy

$$F_B=\rho gV_{displaced}$$

The buoyant force acts through the centroid of the displaced volume.
