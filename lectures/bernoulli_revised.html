Learning Objectives

  • Derive Bernoulli from Newton's second law along a streamline
  • Interpret pressure, velocity, and elevation head
  • Apply continuity and Bernoulli to an engineering problem
  • State assumptions and recognize invalid applications
Read the outcomes briefly. Emphasize that students will use the concepts in calculations, not only recognize definitions.

Inviscid Particle Balance Along a Streamline

Tangential acceleration in steady flow:

$$a_s=V\frac{dV}{ds}$$

Pressure and gravity forces per unit mass give:

$$V\frac{dV}{ds}=-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dp}{ds}-g\frac{dz}{ds}$$
Identify the streamline coordinate s. Explain why viscous shear is neglected in Euler's equation. Connect V dV/ds to the convective acceleration from the previous part.

Integrating Euler Along a Streamline

For constant density:

$$\frac{dp}{\rho}+V\,dV+g\,dz=0$$

$$\frac{p}{\rho}+\frac{V^2}{2}+gz=C$$

Between points 1 and 2:

$$\frac{p_1}{\rho}+\frac{V_1^2}{2}+gz_1=\frac{p_2}{\rho}+\frac{V_2^2}{2}+gz_2$$
Show the three elementary integrals. State that the constant is generally different on different streamlines in rotational flow.

Head Form and Physical Interpretation

$$H=\frac{p}{\rho g}+\frac{V^2}{2g}+z$$

Pressure head

\(p/(\rho g)\)

Velocity head

\(V^2/(2g)\)

Elevation head

\(z\)

Every term has dimensions of length and represents mechanical energy per unit weight.

Use dimensional homogeneity as a quick check. Distinguish pressure head from pressure energy language if students are uncomfortable with the latter.

Assumptions and Scope

  • Steady flow
  • Constant density
  • Negligible viscous work along the selected streamline
  • Gravity is the relevant conservative body force
  • No pump or turbine work in the basic form
  • Applied between points on the same streamline unless the flow is irrotational
Require students to list assumptions before calculating. Clarify that Bernoulli is exact for the ideal model, not a universal formula for every flow.

Interpret Carefully

A higher velocity does not automatically imply lower pressure in every flow.

Along the same streamline, under Bernoulli assumptions and at controlled elevation:

$$V\uparrow\quad\Rightarrow\quad p\downarrow$$
Give counter-context: pressure may be raised by a pump, elevation may change, losses may occur, or the compared points may be on different streamlines.

Worked Application

Combine continuity, boundary conditions, and Bernoulli.

Reservoir Discharge Through a Nozzle

  1. Choose point 1 at the reservoir free surface
  2. Choose point 2 at the nozzle exit
  3. Use gauge pressure: \(p_1=p_2=0\)
  4. For a large reservoir: \(V_1\approx0\)
  5. Set the elevation difference \(z_1-z_2=h\)
Draw the datum and point labels. Make explicit why atmospheric pressure cancels when using gauge pressure.

Reservoir Discharge Solution

Bernoulli from the free surface to the exit:

$$0+0+z_1=0+\frac{V_2^2}{2g}+z_2$$
$$V_2=\sqrt{2gh}$$

Flow rate:

$$Q=A_2V_2$$

Check: ideal discharge. Real flows require a discharge coefficient or loss term.

This is Torricelli's result. Ask which assumptions are most questionable near a real nozzle. Mention contraction and viscous loss without deriving coefficients.

Flow Measurement Examples

  • Venturi meter: continuity plus pressure difference
  • Pitot-static tube: stagnation and static pressure
  • Siphon: pressure and elevation along a connected streamline
Select only one additional example if time remains. Do not treat a list of devices as equivalent to worked problem practice.

From Bernoulli to the Engineering Energy Equation

Real systems may include pumps, turbines, and losses:

$$\frac{p_1}{\rho g}+\alpha_1\frac{V_1^2}{2g}+z_1+h_p-h_t-h_L=\frac{p_2}{\rho g}+\alpha_2\frac{V_2^2}{2g}+z_2$$

The control-volume basis is developed in Lecture 3.

Preview only. Define pump head, turbine head, head loss, and kinetic-energy correction factor. Do not derive them yet.

Lecture 2 Takeaways

  • Material acceleration connects kinematics to dynamics
  • Euler's equation is Newton's second law for an inviscid fluid
  • Bernoulli is the streamline integral under stated assumptions
  • Continuity and Bernoulli are normally used together
  • Always select points, datum, pressure reference, and assumptions explicitly
Exit question: Can Bernoulli be used across a pump without modification? Answer: not in the basic form; shaft work must be included.
